## Consequences (the volume in a long block, measured)
- **One block ERROR per blocked cycle, never rate-limited: 713 characters** with the real redaction helper and 806 unbound (686 for a pending re-run with its batch), i.e. ~100 characters shorter than the 817 le-047 computed for the pre-#907 record; ~1,440 cycles/day is ~1.0 MB/day of those records plus log prefixes.
- **While a pass is PENDING and blocked there is also the rate-limited `drain_held_pending_consolidation` WARNING (~1 per cycle, because `CC_NG_RECALL_WARN_INTERVAL_S` defaults to 60 s = the autosave interval) and its `status` counter: roughly 2 records per cycle in that state.** A pure-cohort block (no deposit yet) is 1 ERROR per cycle.
- **A mid-pass hold costs two ERRORs once** (the shared function's `HELD at a slice boundary` record with the blocker COUNT, and the daemon's consequence record naming how many steps are owed), then the per-cycle block record until the guard clears. A raising slice guard is a failure: the shared function's class-name record plus the daemon's `FAILED ... RE-ARMED` record, per attempt.
- **Without #905 (pre-merge) the daemon says so once per process** (ERROR) and counts each call in `drain_slice_guard_unavailable`; the C3 per-slice guard is then NOT enforced mid-pass and only the start-of-pass whole-graph check applies (C3 stays open until #905 merges).
- A pending batch that stays blocked starves new deposits by design (the tract grows, miniTID keeps appending): that is the 25/250 rule, now without the old loophole where a blocked batch's steps were forfeited.
